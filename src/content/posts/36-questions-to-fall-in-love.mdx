I recently tried the 36 questions with someone, and wanted a simple way to share the experience. The prompts start lightly and become more personal across three sets. Take turns reading each one, and give both people room to answer before moving on. There is no need to race through them.

The questions below are adapted from the task slips in [Arthur Aron and colleagues' 1997 study](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003). The study explored whether structured self-disclosure could help people feel closer; it did not promise that two people would fall in love. [Mandy Len Catron's New York Times essay](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/01/11/style/modern-love-to-fall-in-love-with-anyone-do-this.html) brought the questions to a wider audience, and [The New York Times published the question list](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/01/11/style/36-questions-that-lead-to-love.html).
